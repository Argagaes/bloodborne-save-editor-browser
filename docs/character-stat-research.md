# Character attribute research

Origin minimums and initial blood levels were read from CUSA03173 1.09 CharaInitParam rows 2000–2008: baseVit, baseWil (Endurance), baseStr, baseDex, baseMag (Bloodtinge), baseFai (Arcane). Each origin gives initial level equal to the sum of the six attributes minus 50. The editor calculates a representable level from attribute totals, while allowing values outside origin minima and the normal cap; level can also be edited independently.

The display-only unmodified HP and stamina tables are transcribed from https://www.bloodborne-wiki.com/2015/05/vitality.html and https://www.bloodborne-wiki.com/2015/05/endurance.html. The installed CalcCorrectGraph row 100 has the max HP growth breakpoints (1/15/30/50/99, 400/682/1100/1500/1900), but the editor does not reproduce the curve from those parameters. Rune bonuses and other in-game effects can change maxima. Examples from repository saves show the three stored health and stamina values can diverge, and do not establish how to recalculate all fields reliably when stats, runes, multiplayer state, and transient effects interact. Attribute changes therefore do not rewrite the stored HP or stamina values. Paired in-game saves and further testing are needed before safe automatic recalculation.

## Growth-curve investigation

Paleblood identifies the original function at `0x02314b60` as a CalcCorrectGraph row lookup, not the function that evaluates the curve. Its rewritten character frame update contains a points-regeneration calculation, but does not establish the maximum stamina formula. The repository does not yet identify the growth-curve evaluator; its `tools/refs_to.sh` can search callers when an analyzed Ghidra project and game executable are available.

The installed HP graph's successive adjustment parameters are 1.1, 1.2, -1.2, and -1.1. As a research hypothesis, a [calculator for a related FromSoftware game](https://github.com/ThomasJClark/elden-ring-weapon-calculator) evaluates stages by setting `t = (stat - lowerStat) / (upperStat - lowerStat)`, transforming it to `t ** adjustment` for positive adjustments or `1 - (1 - t) ** -adjustment` for negative ones, then interpolating between the growth values. Applying this hypothesis to the Bloodborne row and flooring the result reproduces 88 of the 93 HP values in the editor's wiki table (vitality 7–99). The five disagreements are vitality 8: 531 vs 530, 14: 659 vs 658, 17: 719 vs 718, 19: 767 vs 766, and 20: 793 vs 792. Another community table reports 659, 719, 767 and 793 for four of those levels, which makes transcription or observation differences plausible, but does not prove the original game's evaluator or its rounding. Game-side verification is required before treating either source as exact.

The stamina graph and the meanings and recalculation rules of all saved HP/stamina fields remain unverified. Next steps are to locate callers of the graph lookup in the original 1.09 executable, verify the evaluator and the graph used for stamina, and compare paired saves created in-game before and after individual stat increases, with and without runes. The editor does not apply the hypothesis to save fields.
